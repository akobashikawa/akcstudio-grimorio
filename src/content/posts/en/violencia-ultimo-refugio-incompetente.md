"Violence is the last refuge of the incompetent."

This line appears in Isaac Asimov's science fiction work Foundation.

For many years I knew it through its Spanish translation:

"La violencia es el último recurso del incompetente." (Last resort.)

But the original says refuge, not resort.

That changes my interpretation a little. Violence becomes a kind of cave or shield, a place you hide when the problem exceeds your competence.

When you are competent, you face a problem with confidence. You listen, you empathize, even with those who think differently, and you are willing to accept that the solution may be different from the one you imagined.

They say every problem contains its own solution. That solution may hold an uncomfortable truth, and you may end up obstructing the search for it, even while believing you are making progress.

As you approach the limit of your competence, you start avoiding the problem, trying to replace it with others you feel more capable of handling.

If you have money, you offer gifts, bonuses.
If you are strong, you push, you hit.
If you have power, you threaten, you overwhelm.
And one way or another, you use violence.

So you pour your effort into this other problem you invented, to believe, or to make others believe, that you have made progress.
But it is the wrong problem.

If you used violence, you are incompetent at solving the real one.
Sooner or later, you will have to step out of the refuge and solve it for real.
